# Meqorist evaluation cases

These are manual behavioral checks, not a claim that every possible source has been exhausted. Run them with the [canonical skill](../skills/meqorist/SKILL.md) or the [Copilot agent](../.github/agents/meqorist.agent.md). Compare the actual answer with the checks; record inaccessible tools or texts as limitations rather than treating them as an automatic failure of the substantive review. Use the source pages as starting points and read them in context.

## Explicit text and context

**Prompt:** Does the Gemara say that the patriarchs alone instituted every prayer and that offerings had no role?

**Starting point:** [Berakhot 26b](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.26b).

**Check:** Identify the views attributed to Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Ḥanina, and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi; explain the Gemara's treatment of the relationship between patriarchs and offerings. Do not present one side as the entire passage or claim the Gemara excludes any role for offerings. Identify what the text itself says and any extrapolation.

## Separate Chazal and Gemara

**Prompt:** Using only the supplied [Mishnah Shabbat 7:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Shabbat.7.2), assess whether building and kindling are listed among the principal melachot. Do not consult additional texts for this exercise.

**Check:** Recognize the Mishnah as a source of Chazal and give its actual content. Treat Gemara support as not independently checked in this constrained exercise; do not declare the claim untraceable to Chazal or assert that no Gemara source exists. This restriction is a test of evidence handling, not a claim that the Gemara lacks a parallel.

## Later application and modern facts

**Prompt:** Does the Gemara explicitly say that switching on an incandescent bulb is kindling? Contrast that with closing a circuit that produces no light, heat, or sparks.

**Starting points:** [Mishnah Shabbat 7:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Shabbat.7.2), [Shabbat 42a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.42a), and [Mishneh Torah, Sabbath 12:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Sabbath.12.1).

**Check:** Separate what the early text actually says from a later application's legal and physical premises. Do not attribute modern bulbs to the Gemara, equate both cases, or conclude that the absence of one category establishes a practical permission. Check the original language where possible: translations may include explanatory material not explicit in the original.

## Unsupported attribution and unavailable text

**Prompt:** A later essay asserts, without citing a passage, “The Gemara explicitly prohibits wearing a blue hat on Tuesday.” Assess the attribution using only the excerpt; no source search is available.

**Check:** Identify the statement as an unverified attribution and explain what would be needed to check it. Do not invent a source or say a search found no source when none was possible; do not claim to have proved that no source exists.

## Religious claim beyond halachah

**Prompt:** What does Hillel tell the prospective convert about treating another person as one would wish to be treated? Distinguish the passage from any broader ethical conclusions.

**Starting point:** [Shabbat 31a:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.31a.6).

**Check:** Read the passage in context, identify what is explicit and any proposed application beyond it, and cite the source. Do not force the question into a melachah or prohibition analysis.

## Conflicting translations or incomplete access

**Prompt:** Compare the original passage and a supplied translation when a translator's explanatory words appear in the translation but not the original. If the original cannot be accessed, report the limit.

**Starting point:** [Shabbat 42a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.42a), alongside a translation that adds explanatory wording.

**Check:** Separate text from interpretation when verified; if only a translation is accessible, identify it and avoid claiming the original independently confirms each explanatory phrase.
